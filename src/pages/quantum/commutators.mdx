The commutator of two operators is defined as

$$
[A,B] = AB - BA
$$

For position and momentum,

$$
[x,p] = i\hbar
$$

If two operators commute, then

$$
[A,B] = 0
$$

This often means they can be simultaneously diagonalized in an appropriate setting.

<Derivation>
  <span slot="summary">Show derivation of $[x,p] = i\hbar$</span>

Let the momentum operator act as

$$
p = -i\hbar \frac{d}{dx}.
$$

Then acting on a test function $\psi(x)$,

$$
[x,p]\psi = x(p\psi) - p(x\psi).
$$

Compute each term:

$$
x(p\psi) = x\left(-i\hbar \frac{d\psi}{dx}\right)
= -i\hbar x \frac{d\psi}{dx},
$$

and

$$
p(x\psi) = -i\hbar \frac{d}{dx}(x\psi)
= -i\hbar \left(\psi + x\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right).
$$

So

$$
[x,p]\psi
= -i\hbar x \frac{d\psi}{dx}
- \left[-i\hbar \left(\psi + x\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right)\right].
$$

This simplifies to

$$
[x,p]\psi = i\hbar \psi.
$$

Therefore,

$$
[x,p] = i\hbar.
$$
</Derivation>
